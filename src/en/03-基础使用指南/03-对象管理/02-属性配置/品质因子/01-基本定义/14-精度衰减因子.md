# Dilution of Precision

## 1. Definition

Dilution of Precision (DOP) is a class of quantitative metrics that measure the geometric conditions experienced by a user during Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) measurements. It is also referred to as DOP value or precision factor, and reflects the impact of the **spatial distribution** of navigation satellites on the positioning accuracy of a ground target.

The DOP value depends on the distribution of navigation satellites in the user's field of view, and reflects the amplification effect of the geometric configuration formed between the user and the satellites on ranging errors. Under the same ranging accuracy conditions:

- A smaller DOP value indicates a more reasonable spatial distribution of resources and better positioning geometry;
- A larger DOP value indicates poorer stability of the spatial geometry formed between the analysis object and the positioning resources, greater amplification of ranging errors, and lower achievable positioning and navigation accuracy.

> **Note**
>
> DOP is a dimensionless indicator that reflects only the geometric conditions. It does not directly include factors such as ranging noise, clock bias models, equipment accuracy, or environmental errors.

## 2. Configuration Interface

The interface mainly includes the following areas:

1. **Definition**: Sets the statistic, DOP sub-method, computation mode, analysis step size, and invalid value handling;

## 3. Computational Principle

### 3.1 Distance Between Resource and Analysis Object

At each analysis time, all positioning resources that satisfy the coverage conditions are first obtained, and the distance between the $i$-th resource and the analysis object is calculated:

$$
\rho_i=
\sqrt{
(x_{s,i}-x_r)^2+
(y_{s,i}-y_r)^2+
(z_{s,i}-z_r)^2
}
$$

where:

- $(x_{s,i},y_{s,i},z_{s,i})$ is the position of the $i$-th positioning resource;
- $(x_r,y_r,z_r)$ is the position of the analysis object;
- $\rho_i$ is the distance between the $i$-th resource and the analysis object.

The resource positions and the analysis object position must be expressed in the same coordinate system.

### 3.2 Geometry Matrix

The geometry matrix is constructed from the unit line‑of‑sight vectors from the resources to the analysis object:

$$
H=
\begin{bmatrix}
\dfrac{x_r-x_{s,1}}{\rho_1} &
\dfrac{y_r-y_{s,1}}{\rho_1} &
\dfrac{z_r-z_{s,1}}{\rho_1} & 1 \\[6pt]
\dfrac{x_r-x_{s,2}}{\rho_2} &
\dfrac{y_r-y_{s,2}}{\rho_2} &
\dfrac{z_r-z_{s,2}}{\rho_2} & 1 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
\dfrac{x_r-x_{s,n}}{\rho_n} &
\dfrac{y_r-y_{s,n}}{\rho_n} &
\dfrac{z_r-z_{s,n}}{\rho_n} & 1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

where $n$ is the number of positioning resources participating in the computation at the current time. The first three columns represent the geometric relationships in the three position directions, and the fourth column represents the influence coefficient of the receiver clock bias on the pseudorange observations.

### 3.3 Geometric Covariance Matrix

Computed from the geometry matrix:

$$
Q=(H^{T}H)^{-1}
$$

Let its diagonal elements be:

$$
Q=
\begin{bmatrix}
q_{xx} & \cdots & \cdots & \cdots\\
\cdots & q_{yy} & \cdots & \cdots\\
\cdots & \cdots & q_{zz} & \cdots\\
\cdots & \cdots & \cdots & q_{tt}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Then $q_{xx}$, $q_{yy}$, and $q_{zz}$ represent the geometric variance coefficients in the three position directions, and $q_{tt}$ represents the geometric variance coefficient in the time or clock bias direction.

> **Computation Conditions**
>
> A full DOP computation requires not only that the number of resources is at least 4, but also that the matrix $H$ has full column rank. Even if the resource count meets the requirement, if the line‑of‑sight directions of the resources are too close, $H^{T}H$ may become singular or ill‑conditioned, resulting in no valid DOP or an excessively large DOP value.

### 3.4 Computation of Various DOP Types

| Method | Name | Formula | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| GDOP | Geometric Dilution of Precision | $\sqrt{q_{xx}+q_{yy}+q_{zz}+q_{tt}}$ | Comprehensively reflects the geometric precision of 3D position and clock bias |
| PDOP | Position Dilution of Precision | $\sqrt{q_{xx}+q_{yy}+q_{zz}}$ | Reflects the geometric precision of the 3D position solution |
| HDOP | Horizontal Dilution of Precision | $\sqrt{q_{EE}+q_{NN}}$ | Reflects the geometric precision of the horizontal position solution |
| VDOP | Vertical Dilution of Precision | $\sqrt{q_{UU}}$ | Reflects the geometric precision in the elevation or vertical direction |
| TDOP | Time Dilution of Precision | $\sqrt{q_{tt}}$ | Reflects the geometric precision of the receiver clock bias solution |
| PDOP(3) | 3‑Resource Position Dilution of Precision | Computed from the 3‑parameter geometry matrix excluding the clock bias column | Ignores the clock bias parameter, computing only the 3D position geometric precision |
| HDOP(3) | 3‑Resource Horizontal Dilution of Precision | Computed from the horizontal components of the 3‑parameter covariance matrix | Ignores the clock bias parameter, computing only the horizontal geometric precision |
| VDOP(3) | 3‑Resource Vertical Dilution of Precision | Computed from the vertical components of the 3‑parameter covariance matrix | Ignores the clock bias parameter, computing only the vertical geometric precision |

where:

$$
HDOP^2+VDOP^2=PDOP^2
$$

$$
PDOP^2+TDOP^2=GDOP^2
$$

> **Coordinate Note**
>
> HDOP and VDOP should be computed in the local East‑North‑Up (ENU) coordinate system at the analysis object's location. If the position covariance is initially obtained in the Earth‑Centered, Earth‑Fixed (ECEF) coordinate system, it should be transformed to the local coordinate system before extracting the horizontal and vertical components.

## 4. Parameter Descriptions

### 4.1 Definition Area

| Parameter | Description | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Metric type. When "Dilution of Precision" is selected, the interface displays the dedicated parameters for this metric. | Keep as "Dilution of Precision" |
| Compute | Statistical method applied to the DOP time series over the analysis period. Options include Mean, Maximum, Minimum, and Percent Below. | Choose based on the statistical characteristic of interest for the mission. |
| Method | Selects the specific DOP type, such as GDOP, PDOP, HDOP, VDOP, TDOP, and the 3‑resource types supported by the software. | Choose GDOP for comprehensive positioning; PDOP if only position is of concern; HDOP or VDOP for horizontal or vertical analyses, respectively. |
| Type (Resource Selection) | Sets the set of positioning resources used for DOP computation at each time. Options include Over‑Determined, Best‑4, and Best‑N. | Choose "Over‑Determined" when many resources are available and all should be used. |
| Step | The time interval between two consecutive computation instants. | A smaller step provides higher temporal resolution but increases computational load. |
| Invalid Value | Sets the handling method for cases such as insufficient resources or non‑invertible matrices. | Generally choose "Ignore" to prevent invalid samples from distorting statistical results. |

### 4.2 Statistics

Assume that over the analysis period, a total of $m$ valid DOP samples are obtained:

$$
d_1,d_2,\ldots,d_m
$$

The statistical methods are defined as follows.

#### Mean

$$
\overline{DOP}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}d_k
$$

Represents the overall average DOP level over the entire analysis period, suitable for evaluating long‑term or overall geometric conditions. The mean is affected by the analysis step size.

#### Maximum

$$
DOP_{\max}=\max(d_1,d_2,\ldots,d_m)
$$

Represents the worst geometric condition encountered during the analysis period. Used to check for significant degradation during local intervals.

#### Minimum

$$
DOP_{\min}=\min(d_1,d_2,\ldots,d_m)
$$

Represents the best geometric condition encountered during the analysis period.

#### Percent Below

Given a percentage $X\%$, the software returns a value $V$ such that approximately $X\%$ of the valid DOP values over the analysis period are no greater than $V$.

Sort the valid DOP samples in ascending order:

$$
d_{(1)}\leq d_{(2)}\leq \cdots \leq d_{(m)}
$$

Then "Percent Below" corresponds to a quantile of the DOP sequence. For example, if set to $95\%$, the output value $V$ indicates that for approximately $95\%$ of the valid analysis time:

$$
DOP\leq V
$$

This statistic is suitable for evaluating the positioning geometry quality during "most of the time," avoiding the influence of a few transient outliers when only the maximum value is used.

### 4.3 Computation Mode

#### Over‑Determined

Uses all available coverage resources at the current time to compute DOP.

- Full GDOP, PDOP, HDOP, VDOP, and TDOP typically require at least 4 valid resources;
- PDOP(3), HDOP(3), and VDOP(3) require at least 3 valid resources;
- When the number of resources exceeds the minimum requirement, adding more resources with different geometric directions generally helps improve the positioning geometry.

This mode utilizes all available resources and is suitable for overall system capability assessment.

#### Best‑4

Computes DOP using the best four resources from the current coverage resource set. The combination is typically selected based on minimizing a specified DOP metric.

This mode is suitable for scenarios where only 4 resources can be used simultaneously, or for evaluating the performance of the best 4‑resource combination.

#### Best‑N

Selects the $N$ resources with the best geometric conditions from the current resource set for computation.

- For full DOP types, $N$ is generally not less than 4;
- For PDOP(3), HDOP(3), and VDOP(3), when GDOP or TDOP is not used as the selection metric, $N$ can be set to 3;
- When "Best‑N" is selected, the $N$ value should be entered in the parameter field that appears in the interface.

This mode is suitable for analyzing the impact of different numbers of available resources on geometric precision when the number of receiver channels is limited.

### 4.4 Invalid Value

The following situations may produce invalid DOP:

- Insufficient resources satisfying the coverage conditions at the current time;
- The geometry matrix is not full rank;
- The line‑of‑sight directions of multiple resources are too concentrated, causing the matrix to be non‑invertible or severely ill‑conditioned;
- Position data for resources or the analysis object is missing;
- Coordinate transformation fails.

When the **Invalid Value** option is set to "Ignore," invalid instants are not included in the computation of Mean, Maximum, Minimum, or Percent Below.

> **Note**
>
> Statistical results should be interpreted together with the number of valid samples or the percentage of valid time. If the number of valid samples is very small, even a small computed mean DOP does not guarantee good positioning conditions throughout the entire analysis period.

### 4.5 Contours

When **Show Contours** is checked, the software can map the selected statistic to the analysis region, displaying DOP levels at different locations using color distributions.

Clicking the **Contours…** button allows further configuration of the contour levels and display parameters. Since smaller DOP values indicate better performance, the contour map should be interpreted in conjunction with the level attributes, and not solely based on color depth.
