# Visibility Constraints

## 1. Functional Description

Visibility Constraints compute geometric, kinematic, or illumination parameters between coverage resources and the analysis object during coverage intervals, and generate Figure of Merit results based on the selected statistical methods.

This metric is computed only when the coverage resources satisfy the established visibility conditions. When multiple coverage resources are available at the same time, the software first aggregates the constraint parameters from each resource according to the **Across Assets** setting, and then applies the **Compute** setting to produce statistics over the entire analysis period.

> **Note**
>
> "Available coverage resources" refers to satellites, aircraft, ships, vehicles, sensors, or chains that satisfy line‑of‑sight occlusion, sensor field‑of‑view, operating range, and other access conditions at the current time, and can participate in the computation for the current analysis object or grid point.

---

## 2. Constraint Types

### 2.1 Altitude

Represents the height of the constraint‑associated object above the central body's surface:

$$
h=R_{\mathrm{obj}}-R_{\mathrm{C,local}}
$$

where:

- $R_{\mathrm{obj}}$ is the distance from the object to the center of the central body;
- $R_{\mathrm{C,local}}$ is the local radius of the central body in the object's direction.

This parameter can be used to analyze whether the object lies within specified minimum or maximum altitude ranges.

### 2.2 Angular Rate

Represents the rate of change of the line‑of‑sight direction from the coverage resource to the analysis object, reflecting how fast the relative line‑of‑sight rotates between the two objects:

$$
\omega_{\mathrm{LOS}}
=
\frac{\left\|\boldsymbol r\times\boldsymbol v\right\|}{R^2}
$$

where:

- $\boldsymbol r$ is the position vector of the coverage resource relative to the analysis object;
- $\boldsymbol v$ is the relative velocity vector;
- $R=\|\boldsymbol r\|$ is the distance between the two objects.

A larger angular rate means the target moves faster across the field of view, imposing higher demands on sensor tracking or communication link pointing capabilities.

### 2.3 Azimuth Angle

Represents the horizontal direction angle from the analysis object to the coverage resource. In the object's local coordinate system, it is typically measured clockwise from local north.

If the relative position components in the object's reference frame are $(r_x,r_y,r_z)$, the azimuth angle can be expressed as:

$$
Az=\operatorname{atan2}(r_y,r_x)
$$

The specific zero‑direction and positive direction are determined by the local reference frame adopted by the object.

### 2.4 Azimuth Rate

Represents the time rate of change of the azimuth angle:

$$
\dot{Az}
=
\frac{r_xv_y-r_yv_x}{r_x^2+r_y^2}
$$

where $v_x$ and $v_y$ are the relative velocity components along the corresponding axes.

### 2.5 Elevation Angle

Represents the elevation angle of the coverage resource above the local horizontal plane of the analysis object. When the elevation angle is greater than $0$, the resource is above the local horizontal plane; when less than $0$, it is below.

$$
El=
\arctan
\left(
\frac{r_z}{\sqrt{r_x^2+r_y^2}}
\right)
$$

### 2.6 Elevation Rate

Represents the time rate of change of the elevation angle, reflecting the target's angular motion speed in the vertical direction. Its computation is determined by both relative position and relative velocity.

A larger absolute value of the elevation rate indicates faster vertical movement of the target within the sensor's field of view.

### 2.7 LOS Lunar Exclusion

Represents the angle between the line‑of‑sight direction (pointing to the coverage resource or target object) and the Moon direction:

$$
\theta_{\mathrm{Moon}}
=
\arccos
\left(
\frac{
\boldsymbol l\cdot\boldsymbol r_{\mathrm{Moon}}
}{
\|\boldsymbol l\|
\|\boldsymbol r_{\mathrm{Moon}}\|
}
\right)
$$

where:

- $\boldsymbol l$ is the line‑of‑sight vector from the analysis object to the coverage resource or target object;
- $\boldsymbol r_{\mathrm{Moon}}$ is the position vector of the Moon relative to the analysis object.

This parameter is used to determine whether the line‑of‑sight satisfies Moon avoidance requirements. When the angle is smaller than a specified threshold, the access is typically considered invalid.

### 2.8 LOS Solar Exclusion

Represents the angle between the line‑of‑sight direction (pointing to the coverage resource or target object) and the Sun direction:

$$
\theta_{\mathrm{Sun}}
=
\arccos
\left(
\frac{
\boldsymbol l\cdot\boldsymbol r_{\mathrm{Sun}}
}{
\|\boldsymbol l\|
\|\boldsymbol r_{\mathrm{Sun}}\|
}
\right)
$$

This parameter is primarily used to prevent strong sunlight from entering the sensor field of view, or to evaluate the impact of the Sun direction on observation tasks.

### 2.9 Lunar Elevation Angle

Represents the apparent elevation angle of the Moon relative to the analysis object. For fixed objects such as ground stations, this can be interpreted as the elevation angle of the Moon direction above the local horizontal plane.

A larger lunar elevation angle means the Moon is higher in the local sky; when the elevation angle is less than $0$, the Moon is below the local horizon.

### 2.10 Sun Elevation Angle

Represents the apparent elevation angle of the Sun relative to the analysis object. For ground‑based objects, this can be interpreted as the elevation angle of the Sun direction above the local horizontal plane.

The Sun elevation angle can be used to determine day/night status, solar illumination conditions, and the impact of sunlight on observation tasks.

### 2.11 Propagation Delay

Represents the time required for a signal to propagate between the coverage resource and the analysis object:

$$
t_{\mathrm{delay}}=\frac{R}{c}
$$

where:

- $R$ is the spatial distance between the two objects;
- $c=299\,792\,458\ \mathrm{m/s}$ is the speed of light.

Propagation delay is commonly used in communication link, command uplink, and data downlink analyses.

### 2.12 Range

Represents the spatial straight‑line distance between the coverage resource and the analysis object:

$$
R=
\sqrt{r_x^2+r_y^2+r_z^2}
$$

This parameter is also referred to as slant range, and can be used to constrain sensor operating range or communication link coverage.

### 2.13 Range Rate

Represents the time rate of change of the distance between the coverage resource and the analysis object, i.e., the relative velocity along the line‑of‑sight direction:

$$
\dot R
=
\frac{\boldsymbol r\cdot\boldsymbol v}{R}
$$

- $\dot R>0$ indicates the distance between the two objects is increasing;
- $\dot R<0$ indicates the distance is decreasing;
- $\dot R=0$ indicates the distance is near an extremum at that instant.

## 3. Across Assets Computation

Assume that at a given time there are $m$ available coverage resources, and their selected constraint parameters are respectively:

$$
q_1(t),q_2(t),\ldots,q_m(t)
$$

The software aggregates the results from multiple resources into a single Figure of Merit value $q(t)$ at that time according to the **Across Assets** setting.

| Across Assets Method | Formula | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Mean | $\displaystyle q(t)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}q_i(t)$ | Computes the mean of the constraint parameter across all available resources at the current time. |
| Maximum | $\displaystyle q(t)=\max_i q_i(t)$ | Takes the maximum value among all available resources at the current time. |
| Minimum | $\displaystyle q(t)=\min_i q_i(t)$ | Takes the minimum value among all available resources at the current time. |
| Sum | $\displaystyle q(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}q_i(t)$ | Sums the constraint parameters across all available resources at the current time. |

---

## 4. Statistics

Over the analysis period, the software samples at the specified **Step**, producing a time series after across‑assets processing:

$$
q(t_1),q(t_2),\ldots,q(t_n)
$$

The **Compute** parameter is used to generate statistics from this time series.

| Statistical Method | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mean | Computes the mean of all valid samples over the analysis period. |
| Maximum | Takes the maximum value of the valid time series over the analysis period. |
| Minimum | Takes the minimum value of the valid time series over the analysis period. |
| Percent Below | Given a percentage $X\%$, returns a value $V$ such that the selected constraint parameter is no greater than $V$ for $X\%$ of the valid analysis time. |
| Percent Above | Given a percentage $X\%$, returns a value $V$ such that the selected constraint parameter is no less than $V$ for $X\%$ of the valid analysis time. |
| Total | Sums the valid samples over the analysis period. This result is affected by the analysis step size. |

The mean can be expressed as:

$$
\overline q
=
\frac{1}{n}
\sum_{k=1}^{n}q(t_k)
$$

The maximum and minimum are respectively:

$$
q_{\max}
=
\max_{1\leq k\leq n}q(t_k)
$$

$$
q_{\min}
=
\min_{1\leq k\leq n}q(t_k)
$$
