# Time Average Gap

## 1. Definition

The Time Average Gap is the probability of being inside a coverage gap at any arbitrary instant, characterized as a function of the gap durations weighted by their respective lengths.

- A larger Time Average Gap indicates that longer continuous gaps account for a higher proportion of the analysis period, implying better coverage continuity.
- A smaller Time Average Gap indicates that gaps are generally shorter or more fragmented, implying relatively weaker continuous coverage capability.

> **Note**
>
> The Time Average Gap has dimensions of time, and its unit is the same as that used for gap durations and the total analysis duration.

## 2. Computation Method

Assume that over the entire analysis period, a total of $n$ valid coverage gaps are formed. The duration of the $i$-th gap is:

$$
dur_i=t_i^{\mathrm{end}}-t_i^{\mathrm{start}}
$$

The total analysis duration is:

$$
dur_{\mathrm{Analy}}
=t_{\mathrm{Analy}}^{\mathrm{end}}
-t_{\mathrm{Analy}}^{\mathrm{start}}
$$

The Time Average Gap is then defined as:

$$
V=
\frac{
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}dur_i^2
}{
dur_{\mathrm{Analy}}
}
$$

where:

- $V$ is the Time Average Gap;
- $dur_i$ is the duration of the $i$-th valid coverage gap;
- $n$ is the number of valid coverage gaps within the analysis period;
- $dur_{\mathrm{Analy}}$ is the total analysis duration.

This formula can also be written as:

$$
V=
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\left(
\frac{dur_i}{dur_{\mathrm{Analy}}}
\right)dur_i
$$

where

$$
\dfrac{dur_i}{dur_{\mathrm{Analy}}}
$$

represents the time‑weighted proportion of the $i$-th gap relative to the total analysis duration. Gaps with longer durations contribute more to the final result.
