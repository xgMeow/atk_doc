# Receiver

Supported objects for receivers: vehicles, satellites, sensors, submarines, ships, missiles, launch vehicles, ground stations, and aircraft.

---

## Functional Description

A receiver models the receiving end of a communication system. Parameters such as frequency, antenna, transmission line, low noise amplifier, system noise temperature, polarization, demodulation scheme, and filters describe the receiving characteristics of the signal and provide inputs for communication link analysis.

Receiver properties include three modules: **Basic Properties** , **Constraints** , and **RF** .

Three receiver models are supported. On the **Basic - Definition** page of the receiver properties, select the desired model using **Type** at the top.

| Model Type | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Simple Receiver Model | Directly specifies the figure of merit (G/T) to describe the receiving capability at the receiving end. |
| Medium Receiver Model | Specifies receive gain and system noise temperature separately to describe the receiving capability at the receiving end. |
| Complex Receiver Model | Specifies system noise temperature and determines antenna gain and its directionality through an antenna model. |

## Model Selection and Page Layout

The three models differ primarily in how receiving capability and antenna characteristics are defined. Their **Demodulator** , **Filter** , and **Additional Gains and Losses** settings are configured in the same way.

| Setting | Simple Receiver Model | Medium Receiver Model | Complex Receiver Model |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Frequency | Set in **Model Specifications** . | Set in **Model Specifications** . | Set in **Model Specifications** . |
| Receiving capability | Enter **G/T** . | Enter **Gain** and determine the value together with **System Noise Temperature** . | Gain is determined by the antenna model and used together with **System Noise Temperature** . |
| Antenna design frequency | No separate setting. | No separate setting. | Displayed in **Model Specifications** . |
| Transmission line and LNA parameters | No separate setting. | Configure **Antenna to LNA Line Loss** , **LNA** , and **LNA to Receiver Line Loss** in **Model Specifications** . | Configure **Antenna to LNA Line Loss** , **LNA** , and **LNA to Receiver Line Loss** in the top-level **Model Specifications** . |
| System noise temperature | No separate **System Noise Temperature** page; its effect is already reflected in G/T. | Set on the **System Noise Temperature** page. | Set on the **System Noise Temperature** page. |

| Polarization | Set in the **Antenna Polarization** area of **Model Specifications** . | Set in the **Antenna Polarization** area of **Model Specifications** . | Set in **Antenna - Polarization** . |

The page layout of the three models is as follows:

- The **Simple Receiver Model** includes **Model Specifications** , **Demodulator** , **Filter** , and **Additional Gains and Losses** .
- The **Medium Receiver Model** adds **System Noise Temperature** on top of the pages above.
- The **Complex Receiver Model** adds **Antenna** on top of the medium model; the **Antenna** page contains three subpages: **Model Specifications** , **Polarization** , and **Pointing** .

Select the Simple Receiver Model when the receiving-end G/T is known; select the Medium Receiver Model when receive gain and system noise temperature are known; select the Complex Receiver Model when the effects of the antenna pattern and mounting pointing on communication need to be considered.

## Simple Receiver Model

Select **Simple Receiver Model** under **Type** . The pages include **Model Specifications** , **Demodulator** , **Filter** , and **Additional Gains and Losses** .

![Simple Receiver Model interface](../../../../zh/03-基础使用指南/03-对象管理/02-属性配置/media/接收器/update2601-108.png)

**Model Specifications** is used to set the receive frequency, the figure of merit, and antenna polarization.

| Parameter | Meaning | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frequency | Carrier frequency of the received signal. | Enter the receive frequency, and select the corresponding frequency unit using the unit box on the right; when **Auto Track** is selected, it is determined by the software. |
| G/T (dB/K) | Ratio of the receive antenna gain to the equivalent noise temperature of the receiving system, which describes the receiving capability at the receiving end. | Directly enter the known G/T, in dB/K . |
| Antenna Polarization | Polarization characteristics of the received signal. | Select **Use** as needed and configure the polarization type and related parameters. See **Antenna Polarization** for details. |

G/T, receive gain, and system noise temperature satisfy:

$$
G/T=G_r-T_n
$$

Here, $G_r$ is the receive antenna gain in dBi; $T_n$ is the equivalent noise temperature of the receiving system in dB(K); and G/T is in dB/K. The Simple Receiver Model directly uses the entered G/T, so receive gain and system noise temperature do not need to be entered separately.

The settings in **Filter** and **Additional Gains and Losses** also affect the receiving-end power results in link calculations. Therefore, avoid counting the same loss in both G/T and other settings.

## Medium Receiver Model

Select **Medium Receiver Model** under **Type** . The pages include **Model Specifications** , **System Noise Temperature** , **Demodulator** , **Filter** , and **Additional Gains and Losses** .

![Medium Receiver Model interface](../../../../zh/03-基础使用指南/03-对象管理/02-属性配置/media/接收器/update2601-109.png)

In **Model Specifications** , the Medium Receiver Model replaces the **G/T** input of the simple model with **Gain** plus transmission line and low noise amplifier parameters. All other settings are configured in the same way.

| Parameter | Meaning | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frequency | Carrier frequency of the received signal. | Enter the receive frequency and select the corresponding unit; when **Auto Track** is selected, it is determined by the software. |
| Gain (dB) | Isotropic gain of the receiving antenna. | Enter the receive antenna gain; it is used together with system noise temperature to determine G/T. |
| Antenna to LNA Line Loss | Loss of the transmission line between the receiving antenna and the low noise amplifier. | Enter the loss of this transmission line according to the equipment parameters. |
| LNA | Gain introduced by the low noise amplifier. | Enter the gain of the low noise amplifier. |
| LNA to Receiver Line Loss | Loss of the transmission line between the low noise amplifier and the receiver. | Enter the loss of this transmission line according to the equipment parameters. |
| Antenna Polarization | Polarization characteristics of the received signal. | Configure in the same way as for the simple model. See **Antenna Polarization** for details. |

This model uses the specified receive gain and the noise temperature given on the **System Noise Temperature** page to determine G/T. Select the Complex Receiver Model when an antenna pattern is needed to describe gain in different directions.

## Complex Receiver Model

Select **Complex Receiver Model** under **Type** . The pages include **Model Specifications** , **Antenna** , **System Noise Temperature** , **Demodulator** , **Filter** , and **Additional Gains and Losses** .

![Complex Receiver Model interface](../../../../zh/03-基础使用指南/03-对象管理/02-属性配置/media/接收器/update2601-110.png)

The Complex Receiver Model describes receiving characteristics through an antenna model. **Gain** and **Antenna Polarization** are no longer configured separately in the top-level **Model Specifications** ; polarization settings are located in **Antenna - Polarization** .

### Model Specifications

| Parameter | Meaning | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frequency | Carrier frequency of the received signal. | Enter the receive frequency and select the corresponding unit; when **Auto Track** is selected, it is determined by the software. |
| Antenna Design Frequency | Design frequency of the antenna in use, reflecting the frequency configuration of the antenna model. | This value is read-only here and cannot be modified directly; antenna parameters are set in the corresponding antenna configuration. |
| Antenna to LNA Line Loss | Loss of the transmission line between the receiving antenna and the low noise amplifier. | Enter the loss of this transmission line according to the equipment parameters. |
| LNA | Gain introduced by the low noise amplifier. | Enter the gain of the low noise amplifier. |
| LNA to Receiver Line Loss | Loss of the transmission line between the low noise amplifier and the receiver. | Enter the loss of this transmission line according to the equipment parameters. |

**Frequency** is the carrier frequency of the received signal, while **Antenna Design Frequency** is the design frequency of the antenna model. They have different meanings and should be checked separately.

### Antenna

Open the **Antenna** page and select the antenna configuration method using **Reference Type** . This page contains three subpages: **Model Specifications** , **Polarization** , and **Pointing** .

- **Embedded**: Define an antenna model within the current receiver and configure its polarization and pointing.
- **Linked**: Reference an existing antenna in the current object and view **Antenna Name** and the read-only antenna model parameters in **Model Specifications** . **Antenna Name** displays only the currently linked antenna; to modify the antenna model parameters, open the properties of the corresponding antenna object.

**Pointing** can be configured within the current receiver only when **Reference Type** is **Embedded** .

**Antenna - Model Specifications** corresponds to the **Basic - Definition** page of an antenna object, and its **Type** can be set to Gaussian Antenna, Isotropic Antenna, Parabolic Antenna, or External Antenna. For detailed descriptions of antenna types, model parameters, and pointing, see the [Antenna User Guide](./天线.md).

**Antenna - Polarization** is used to configure the polarization characteristics of the received signal. Its parameters are the same as those in **Antenna Polarization** under **Model Specifications** for the simple and medium models. See **Antenna Polarization** in this document for details.

## System Noise Temperature

**System Noise Temperature** determines the equivalent noise temperature used by the receiving system in link calculations. It applies to the Medium Receiver Model and the Complex Receiver Model. The Simple Receiver Model has no such page; its effect is already reflected in the entered G/T.

The page provides two ways to determine the value: select **Constant (K)** to enter the noise temperature directly, or select **Compute** to calculate it from the temperature and noise parameters of the **Antenna to LNA Line** , **LNA** , and **LNA to Receiver Line** segments.

| Parameter | Meaning | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constant (K) | Directly specified system noise temperature. | After selecting this option, enter the equivalent noise temperature of the receiving system, in K. |
| Compute | Calculates the system noise temperature from the parameters of each segment. | After selecting this option, configure the parameters of each segment below. |
| Antenna to LNA Line - Temperature (K) | Physical temperature of the transmission line between the receiving antenna and the low noise amplifier. | Enter the value according to the actual installation environment, in K. |
| LNA - Noise (dB) | Noise figure of the low noise amplifier. | Enter the value according to the equipment parameters, in dB. |
| LNA - Temperature (K) | Physical temperature of the low noise amplifier. | Enter the value according to the actual operating conditions, in K. |
| LNA to Receiver Line - Temperature (K) | Physical temperature of the transmission line between the low noise amplifier and the receiver. | Enter the value according to the actual installation environment, in K. |
| Temperature (K) | Value of the system noise temperature and the way it is specified. | Select **Constant (K)** to enter it directly, or select **Compute** to have the software calculate it. |

The equivalent noise temperature of the receiving system is formed by the internal noise temperature and the external noise temperature referred to the input:

$$
T_n=T_i+\frac{T_a}{L_{rf}}
$$

Here, $T_n$ is the equivalent noise temperature of the receiving system, $T_i$ is the internal noise temperature of the receiving system, $T_a$ is the external noise temperature of the receiving system, and $L_{rf}$ is the feeder loss of the receiving system; $T_a/L_{rf}$ represents the feeder loss to which the external noise is subject at the input of the receiver. All temperatures above are referred to the input of the receiver.

The internal noise temperature consists of the feeder loss noise temperature and the receiver noise temperature:

$$
T_i=T_{rf}+T_r
$$

Here, $T_{rf}$ is the feeder loss noise temperature of the receiving system, and $T_r$ is the receiver noise temperature. The noise temperature caused by transmission line loss is:

$$
T_{rf}=(L_{rf}-1)T_{0, phys}
$$

Here, $T_{0, phys}$ is the physical temperature of the transmission line, generally 290 K for ground equipment and about 300 K for onboard equipment.

The receiver noise temperature can be converted from the noise figure:

$$
T_r=(F_r-1)T_0
$$

Here, $F_r$ is the receiver noise figure, and $T_0$ is the reference noise temperature, generally 290 K.

Once the system noise temperature is determined, the G/T and noise power spectral density used in link calculations are:

$$
G/T=G_r-T_n
$$

$$
N_0=K+T_n
$$

Here, $G_r$ is the receive antenna gain in dBi; $N_0$ is the noise power spectral density; $K$ is the Boltzmann constant, taken as $-228.6\ \mathrm{dBW/HzK}$; and $T_n$ is expressed in dB(K).

## Antenna Polarization

Polarization describes the direction of the electric field vector of an electromagnetic wave and how it varies over time. Polarization matching between the transmitting and receiving ends affects power reception over the communication link.

For the simple and medium models, configure polarization in **Model Specifications - Antenna Polarization** ; for the complex model, configure it in **Antenna - Polarization** .

| Parameter | Meaning | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Use | Whether antenna polarization settings are enabled. | Select this option when polarization characteristics need to be defined, and configure the related parameters. |
| Type | Polarization type of the received signal. | Select linear, horizontal, vertical, left-hand circular, or right-hand circular polarization. |
| Reference Axis | Reference coordinate axis used to define the polarization direction. | For polarization types that require a reference axis, select the X, Y, or Z axis. |
| Polarization Angle(deg) | Angle by which the linear polarization direction deviates from the selected reference direction. | Enter the polarization angle when the corresponding parameter is editable, and check the angle unit. |
| Cross-Polarization Leakage Threshold (dB) | Decision threshold at which the cross-polarization component takes part in link calculations, describing the degree to which the receiving end suppresses the cross-polarization component. | Enter the value according to the cross-polarization characteristics of the receiving antenna, in dB. |

| Polarization Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Linear | The electric field vector oscillates along a fixed direction, which can be defined using the reference axis and polarization angle. |
| Horizontal | The electric field oscillates in the horizontal direction under the reference definition in use. |
| Vertical | The electric field oscillates in the vertical direction under the reference definition in use. |
| Left-Hand Circular | The electric field vector rotates in the left-hand circular polarization sense. |
| Right-Hand Circular | The electric field vector rotates in the right-hand circular polarization sense. |

After selecting the polarization type, configure the parameters that are editable for that type. Interpret the horizontal and vertical directions relative to the selected reference axis; they cannot be directly equated with the horizontal and vertical directions on the screen. **Cross-Polarization Leakage Threshold** is a receiving-end parameter and has the same meaning for linear, circular, and other polarization types.

### Antenna Polarization Loss Calculation

Polarization loss results from a mismatch between the polarization states of the received signal and the receiving antenna. In the following, $L_p$ denotes polarization loss in dB, with positive values consistently representing loss.

#### Linear Polarization

A polarization loss of 3-4 dB needs to be considered. For circular polarization with the same sense of rotation, consider a polarization loss of 0.5-2 dB.

#### Horizontal Polarization

Polarization loss is calculated as:

$$
L_p=-20\log_{10}|\cos\theta|
$$

Here, $\theta$ is the polarization angle between the received signal and the receiving antenna.

#### Vertical Polarization

Polarization loss is calculated as:

$$
L_p=-20\log_{10}|\cos\theta|
$$

Here, $\theta$ is the polarization angle between the received signal and the receiving antenna.

#### Left-Hand Circular Polarization

When the axial ratio of non-ideal circular polarization is taken into account, polarization loss can be expressed as:

$$
L_p
=
-10\log_{10}
\left\{
\frac{1}{2}
\left[
1+
\frac{
\pm 4X_rX_t
+
(1-X_t^2)(1-X_r^2)\cos(2\alpha)
}{
(1+X_t^2)(1+X_r^2)
}
\right]
\right\}
$$

Here, $X_t$ is the axial ratio of the incident wave, and $X_r$ is the axial ratio of the receiving antenna polarization. The axial ratio is the ratio of the electric field amplitudes along the major and minor axes of the polarization ellipse; $\alpha$ is the angle between the major axes of the incident wave polarization ellipse and the receiving antenna polarization ellipse.

The sign in the formula is determined by whether the sense of rotation of the incident wave matches that of the receiving antenna: use "+" when they match and "-" when they are opposite.

#### Right-Hand Circular Polarization

Right-hand circular polarization uses the same axial-ratio loss formula as left-hand circular polarization. The sign in the formula depends on whether the transmitting and receiving polarization senses match.

## Demodulator

**Demodulator** is used to configure the demodulation and decoding scheme for the received signal and the corresponding bit error rate characteristics. All three receiver models are configured in the same way.

### Demodulation Type

| Parameter | Meaning | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Auto-select Demodulator | Whether the software automatically selects the demodulation scheme according to the incoming signal. | When selected, the software sets it automatically; when cleared, **Type** can be selected manually. |
| Type | Demodulation scheme or preset demodulation and coding combination used at the receiving end. | Select a type from the table below. |

| Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| BPSK | Binary phase-shift keying. |
| QPSK | Quadrature phase-shift keying. |
| 8PSK | Eight-phase shift keying. |
| 16PSK | Sixteen-phase shift keying. |
| BPSK-BCH-511-259 | Preset combination of BPSK modulation and BCH coding. |
| BPSK-Conv-4-3-8 | Preset combination of BPSK modulation and convolutional coding. |

Select the appropriate type according to the demodulation and decoding scheme used by the communication system. The types listed above share the settings on this page; switching types does not require additional type-specific parameters. The demodulation and decoding scheme selected at the receiving end should match the modulation and coding scheme used at the transmitting end.

### Modulation/Demodulation and Coding Loss

The modulation scheme mainly affects two aspects: first, the proportion of the transmitted signal power received within the receiver bandwidth, i.e., the bandwidth overlap factor; second, the correspondence between the signal-to-noise ratio $E_b/N_0$ and the bit error rate.

For coding schemes such as BPSK-BCH-511-259 and BPSK-Conv-4-3-8, the bandwidth changes in addition to the bit error rate being affected. The bit error rate of a coding scheme is determined by looking up and interpolating the correspondence between the signal-to-noise ratio and the bit error rate, so the modulation and coding combinations at both ends should be kept in step in a communication task.

## Filter

**Filter** is used to filter the power spectral density of the received signal, describing the spectral changes and corresponding losses as the received signal passes through the filter. All three receiver models have the same page parameters and are configured in the same way.

### Receiver Bandwidth

**Receiver Bandwidth** is used to set the operating bandwidth of the receiver.

| Parameter | Meaning | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bandwidth | Operating bandwidth of the receiver. | Enter the bandwidth value and select the corresponding frequency unit. |
| Auto Scale | Whether the software determines the receiver bandwidth automatically. | Select this option to determine the bandwidth automatically; clear it to specify the bandwidth manually. |

Receiver bandwidth is used to determine the noise power within the operating bandwidth of the receiver and takes part in the calculation of metrics such as $C/N$ and $E_b/N_0$ . **Bandwidth** under **Receiver Bandwidth** is the operating bandwidth of the receiver, which differs in meaning from **Bandwidth** and **Cutoff Frequency** under **Filter Model** ; check them separately.

### Common Parameters

Select **Use** , then choose the filter type and configure its parameters; when this option is cleared, the filter settings are not enabled. Supported types include Bessel, Butterworth, Chebyshev, Gaussian Window, and Raised Cosine.

| Parameter | Meaning | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Use | Whether the filter is enabled. | Select this option when filter modeling is required for the received signal. |
| Type | Filter model in use. | Select a type from the drop-down list, and the page displays the corresponding parameters. |
| Upper Cutoff Frequency | Upper boundary of the filter's spectrum calculation range relative to the carrier frequency. | Enter the upper-frequency boundary and select the unit. |
| Lower Cutoff Frequency | Lower boundary of the filter's spectrum calculation range relative to the carrier frequency. | Enter the lower-frequency boundary and select the unit. |
| Bandwidth | Frequency span between the upper-frequency and lower-frequency boundaries. | Determined by the upper and lower boundaries and displayed as read-only. |
| Insertion Loss(dB) | Fixed signal power loss introduced by inserting the filter. | Enter the value according to the equipment parameters; this differs from the spectral loss caused by the filter's frequency response. |

**Upper Cutoff Frequency** and **Lower Cutoff Frequency** specify the spectral range used; the separate **Cutoff Frequency** setting available for some filters defines their frequency response characteristics. These settings have different meanings.

### Types and Type-Specific Parameters

| Filter Type | Characteristics | Settings in Addition to Common Parameters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bessel | Emphasizes phase and group delay characteristics to describe filter responses with waveform fidelity requirements. | Order and cutoff frequency. |
| Butterworth | Has a flat passband magnitude response. | Order and cutoff frequency. |
| Chebyshev | Achieves a steeper frequency transition by allowing ripple in the magnitude response. | Order, cutoff frequency, and pulse factor. |
| Gaussian Window | Describes filter characteristics using a Gaussian window. | Order and sampling frequency. |
| Raised Cosine | Describes a spectral transition using a raised cosine, commonly used for communication signal shaping. | Roll-off factor and symbol rate. |

| Parameter | Applicable Types | Meaning and Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Order | Bessel, Butterworth, Chebyshev, Gaussian Window | Filter order parameter. Set it according to the design requirements of the selected filter to determine the form of its response. |
| Cutoff Frequency | Bessel, Butterworth, Chebyshev | Characteristic frequency describing the transition of the filter response from the passband to the attenuation region. Enter the corresponding frequency and select the unit. |
| Pulse Factor(dB) | Chebyshev | Controls ripple in the magnitude response of the Chebyshev filter, i.e., the ripple parameter. Set the required ripple magnitude. |
| Sampling Frequency | Gaussian Window | Sampling frequency parameter of the Gaussian Window filter. Set it according to the filter design requirements and select the corresponding unit. |
| Roll-off Factor(%) | Raised Cosine | Controls the spectral transition region and excess bandwidth of the raised cosine response. Enter the value according to the signal shaping requirements. |
| Symbol Rate(Mb/s) | Raised Cosine | Number of modulation symbols transmitted per second, which combines with the roll-off factor to affect the filter bandwidth. Set it according to the symbol rate of the modulated signal, in Mb/s. |

### Calculation Models for the Filter Types

The following basic mathematical forms describe the response characteristics and parameter meanings of each filter type. In the formulas, $f$ represents the frequency offset relative to the carrier frequency. All frequency parameters should use consistent units.

#### Bessel Filter

The frequency response of a Bessel filter can be expressed in the following polynomial form:

$$
H(f)
=
\frac{K_n}
{B_n\left(\frac{f}{f_c}\right)}
$$

Here, $n$ is the filter order, corresponding to **Order** ; $f_c$ is the cutoff frequency, corresponding to **Cutoff Frequency** ; and $B_n(x)$ is the Bessel polynomial.

The recurrence relation for the polynomials is:

$$
B_n(x)
=
(2n-1)B_{n-1}(x)-x^2B_{n-2}(x),
\qquad n\geq 2
$$

The initial conditions and normalization coefficient are:

$$
B_0(x)=1,
\qquad
B_1(x)=1+jx,
\qquad
K_n=B_n(0)
$$

Here, $j$ is the imaginary unit, and the magnitude response is $|H(f)|$.

#### Butterworth Filter

The magnitude response of an $n$th-order Butterworth filter can be expressed as:

$$
H(f)
=
\frac{1}
{\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{f}{f_c}\right)^{2n}}}
$$

Here, $n$ is the filter order, corresponding to **Order** ; $f_c$ is the cutoff frequency, corresponding to **Cutoff Frequency** .

#### Chebyshev Filter

The magnitude response of a Chebyshev filter can be expressed as:

$$
H(f)
=
\frac{1}
{\sqrt{1+E^2C_n^2\left(\frac{f}{f_c}\right)}}
$$

Here, $n$ is the filter order, corresponding to **Order** ; $E$ is a design constant controlling passband ripple, corresponding to **Pulse Factor** ; $C_n(x)$ is the $n$th-order Chebyshev polynomial; and $f_c$ is the cutoff frequency, corresponding to **Cutoff Frequency** .

The Chebyshev polynomials satisfy the following recurrence relation:

$$
C_n(x)
=
2xC_{n-1}(x)-C_{n-2}(x),
\qquad n\geq 2
$$

The initial conditions are:

$$
C_0(x)=1,
\qquad
C_1(x)=x
$$

#### Gaussian Window Filter

The Gaussian Window filter uses a Gaussian function to describe smoothly decaying weights. The standard form of the Gaussian function is:

$$
G(x,y)
=
\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma^2}
\exp\left(
-\frac{x^2+y^2}{2\sigma^2}
\right)
$$

Here, $G(x,y)$ is the Gaussian function; $\sigma$ is the standard deviation, which controls the width of the function's distribution; and $x、y$ are pixel coordinates.

#### Raised Cosine Filter

The Raised Cosine filter consists of a passband, a cosine transition band, and a stopband. Its frequency response is:

$$
H(f)
=
\begin{cases}
T,
&
|f|\leq \dfrac{1-\alpha}{2T},
\\[8pt]
\dfrac{T}{2}
\left[
1+
\cos\left(
\dfrac{\pi T}{\alpha}
\left(
|f|-\dfrac{1-\alpha}{2T}
\right)
\right)
\right],
&
\dfrac{1-\alpha}{2T}
<
|f|
\leq
\dfrac{1+\alpha}{2T},
\\[10pt]
0,
&
|f|>
\dfrac{1+\alpha}{2T}.
\end{cases}
$$

Here, $T$ is the symbol period and is related to the symbol rate $R_s$ by $T=1/R_s$; $\alpha$ is the roll-off factor, with a range of $0\leq\alpha\leq 1$.

## Additional Gains and Losses

**Additional Gains and Losses** supplements the description of additional gains or losses at the receiving end. All three receiver models are configured in the same way.

| Parameter | Meaning | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gain/Loss(dB) | Additional net gain or loss applied to the receiving end beyond the items already modeled separately. | Enter the signed value of the additional gain or loss; positive values represent gain, and negative values represent loss. |

Clearly identify where each gain or loss is accounted for when configuring the settings. A loss already included in **Antenna to LNA Line Loss** , **LNA to Receiver Line Loss** , **Filter** , or **System Noise Temperature** should not be counted again in **Additional Gains and Losses** .

## Configuration Procedure

- Open the receiver properties and select the receiver model type on the **Basic - Definition** page.
- Set the **Frequency** in **Model Specifications** ; enter the **G/T** for the simple model, and the **Gain** plus transmission line and LNA parameters for the medium model.
- When using the medium or complex model, open the **System Noise Temperature** page, select **Constant (K)** or **Compute** , and configure the corresponding temperature and noise parameters.
- When using the complex model, open the **Antenna** page and select **Embedded** or **Linked** ; configure the antenna model and pointing for an embedded antenna, or verify the referenced antenna for a linked antenna.
- Configure polarization characteristics and the **Cross-Polarization Leakage Threshold** in **Model Specifications - Antenna Polarization** or **Antenna - Polarization** according to the selected model.
- In **Demodulator** , determine whether to use **Auto-select Demodulator** , and select the demodulation type.
- In **Filter** , set the **Receiver Bandwidth** , and enable the filter model as needed to configure its common and type-specific parameters.
- Set any additional gains or losses that have not yet been accounted for in **Additional Gains and Losses** .
- Check all parameters and units, then click **Apply** to save the current settings or **OK** to save and close the properties window.
