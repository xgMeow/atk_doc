# Time Constraints

This chapter gives the definitions and conversion relationships of the time bases involved in Temporal Constraints.

## 1 Local Time

Local Time (LT) is measured by the position of the **mean Sun** relative to the local meridian, that is, the sum of Greenwich Mean Time (GMT) and the time offset of the object's longitude relative to the prime meridian ($0^{\circ}$ longitude):

$$
LT=GMT+\Delta_{\mathrm{lon}}
$$

where $\Delta_{\mathrm{lon}}$ is the time offset of the object's longitude relative to the prime meridian (positive east of Greenwich), and its value is derived from the longitude:

$$
\Delta_{\mathrm{lon}}=\frac{\lambda}{15^{\circ}}(\mathrm{h})
$$

where $\lambda$ is the longitude of the object (positive east of Greenwich, negative west of Greenwich), and $15^{\circ}$ of longitude corresponds to a time difference of 1 h. At the same instant, objects at different longitudes have different local times; the Local constraint therefore bounds the access window by the object's local working hours.

## 2 Greenwich Mean Time

Greenwich Mean Time (GMT) is measured by the position of the **mean Sun** relative to the prime meridian. When the mean Sun is at upper transit on the local meridian at longitude $\lambda$:

$$
GMT=12{:}00-\frac{\lambda}{15^{\circ}}(\mathrm{h})
$$

where $\lambda$ is the longitude at which the mean Sun is at upper transit (positive east of Greenwich, negative west of Greenwich), and $15^{\circ}$ of longitude corresponds to a time difference of $1\ \mathrm{h}$.

## 3 Local Apparent Time

Local Apparent Time (LAT) is measured by the position of the **true Sun** relative to the local meridian; it is also called local apparent solar time.
